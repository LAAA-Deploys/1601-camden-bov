Executive Summary

Same Zone, 0.46 Miles Away
1723 S Corinth Ave, a [Q]R3 lot almost the size of this one, has a permit for a 42-unit building
It sold in March 2025 for $2,500,000 ($339.72 per land SF) with 10 rent-stabilized apartments on it. The buyer pulled a demolition permit in December 2025 and a permit for a five-story, 42-unit, 100% affordable building in May 2026, on 7,359 SF.

1601 Camden Avenue is a 7,411 SF corner lot in the [Q]R3-1-O zone in Westwood, just north of Santa Monica Boulevard and east of the 405. It is improved with a 1948 duplex of 3,164 SF, both units registered under the City's Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO).

The value is the land. The site is in a Transit Oriented Incentive Area (Tier 2) under the City's Mixed Income Incentive Program, within half a mile of a major transit stop, in a TCAC/HCD Highest Resource area, and it is one of the City's Lower Income Rezoning Housing Element sites, which allow ministerial approval for qualifying projects. The City's rezoning inventory assumes 19 units here. Five development pathways apply, from keeping the duplex and adding two ADUs to a 100% affordable building of about 40 units. Because the property is owner occupied, a for-sale small-lot subdivision under SB 684 is also possible: five homes with two-car garages, each entered from Massachusetts Avenue. Redevelopment sites nearby sold for a median of $265.52 per land SF from 2025 on. We recommend pricing the property at $2,000,000 ($269.87 per land SF).

SB 684 / SB 1123 Small-Lot Homes

Possible Because the Property Is Owner Occupied
Five for-sale homes, each with its own lot, front door and two-car garage on Massachusetts Avenue
City guidance bars SB 684 and SB 1123 projects from demolishing units "subject to ... the City's Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO), or that have been occupied by tenants within the past 5 years," and from sites with an Ellis Act withdrawal in the past 15 years. This duplex is the owner's home and carries the homeowner's exemption, so no tenant has to leave and no Ellis Act withdrawal is needed. With no tenant in the past five years, and LAHD confirming that the owner-occupied units are exempt from the RSO, a home builder can remove the duplex and subdivide.

SB 684 (2024) lets a builder on a multifamily-zoned lot like this one subdivide into as many as 10 small lots and sell each home separately, with ministerial approval: homes averaging no more than 1,750 SF, 4 ft side and rear setbacks at most and no more than one required parking space per home. SB 1123 (2025) extended it to vacant single-family lots. Because it turns land into for-sale homes, it is often the highest-value use of a lot this size, and the corner makes this one unusually efficient: every home can face Massachusetts Avenue, so the plan needs no shared driveway at all. We modeled it with our SB 1123 underwriting tool, following two small-lot rows built nearby on the same pattern, 11333-11345 W Pearl St (2019) and 11733-11737 W Missouri Ave (2017).

Concept site plan: five attached three-story homes in a row, each on its own 1,482 SF lot (26 x 57 ft) with its front door and a two-car garage on Massachusetts Avenue. The two corner-end homes are 1,499 SF and the three middle homes 1,917 SF, an average of 1,750 SF. Every home fronts a public street, so there is no common driveway, backup aisle or fire lane.

Key Takeaways

1
Redevelopment land here trades at $239 to $340 per SF. The six 2025 and 2026 sales median $265.52 per land SF, with the two-lot Exposition sale at $435.48 as a high outlier. The two [Q]R3 sites within half a mile, both carrying rent-stabilized units, sold higher: $302.32 (1521-1523 S Veteran, 2024) and $339.72 (1723 S Corinth).
2
RSO buildings are selling as sites. Corinth's buyer bought 10 rent-stabilized units, pulled a demolition permit and has a permit for 42 affordable units. Veteran sold with a 47-unit plan on file from 2022, and its duplex now has a demolition permit.
3
The upside is density. At $2,000,000, a buyer pays about $57,000 to $80,000 per unit on a 25 to 35-unit MIIP building, about $105,000 on the 19 units the City's rezoning assumes, and about $50,000 per unit on a 40-unit affordable building.
4
The price needs a developer buyer. Buyers who kept their buildings and added ADUs paid $178 to $252 per land SF (median $211.86). $2,000,000 is a land price, and it holds when the site is marketed to builders with answers on the second unit's occupancy, the fault trace and the [Q] condition in hand.

At $2,000,000 the lot is priced at $269.87 per land SF, right at the median of the six redevelopment sites that sold nearby in 2025 and 2026 ($265.52) and below the two [Q]R3 sites within half a mile, 1521-1523 S Veteran at $302.32 and 1723 S Corinth at $339.72. Both of those carried rent-stabilized units, as this one does, and both had a larger plan on file when they sold; this lot brings a corner location, the City's transit incentives and a Highest Resource address.

The per-unit price on the multifamily pathways supports the same number, and our small-lot home model, possible here because the property is owner occupied, shows a home builder underwriting a 25% margin could pay $1.86M to $2.52M between the base and high exits. Buyers who would keep the duplex have paid less, so the property should be marketed to developers and home builders. The sale is well below the City's Measure ULA threshold of $5,400,000, so no ULA tax applies.
